Asked how it implemented the 2022 OM, HAL sends letters from 2012 and 2016

A 19-page reply, sent through the portal and also by e-mail: a two-page letter, a 2012 HAL circular with the defence and home ministry letters it forwarded (three pages appear twice), an item-wise report sent to the defence ministry in August 2012, and a 2016 circular on Official Language implementation in Hindi and English.

What was asked, and what came back

1Circulars issued under the OM“A copy of the circular / instructions issued well before the said Office Memorandum ... is enclosed as Annexure-1” (a HAL letter of 16 July 2012, with the defence ministry's note of 05.07.2012 and the Department of Official Language OM of 20.06.2012)
2Action taken to use Kannada“The action taken report / compliance report as above is enclosed as Anexure-2”: an item-wise report sent to the defence ministry on 07 August 2012
3Inspection / compliance reportsSame answer as point 2 (one cell covers points 2 and 3); no inspection or audit report
4Corrective action on non-compliance“Since the compliance of the guidelines issued in this regard is being ensured, therefore there is no incident of non-compliance”; refers to point 15 of Annexure-3 (a circular of 25.07.2016)
5Officer responsible for monitoringUnder Rule 12 of the Official Languages Rules, 1976, “the concerned Divisional / Head of Office”, and also “the other concerned officers”

Why it matters

Every document enclosed in answer to questions about the OM of 21.06.2022 dates from 2012 or 2016; none is from after 2022. The ‘compliance report’ is a report sent to the defence ministry on 07.08.2012, which says name boards of all divisions and offices in non-Hindi areas ‘are being put up’ in trilingual form and that instructions were circulated to convert bilingual boards. There is no inspection report, no photograph and no list of offices in Karnataka. Point 15 of the 2016 circular cited for point 4 says banners, boards and similar items must not be made in English only and must be ‘bilingual/trilingual’; the list of check points that circular refers to is not in the reply. The reply's letterhead is in Kannada, Hindi and English.

Letter no. HAL/HR/59/2016 dated 25.07.2016

Subject - Important guidelines regarding implementation of official language

As per the decision taken in the last meeting of the Hindi Guidance Committee constituted at the Headquarters level, held on 02 April 2016, All HAL Official Language Seminar was organized on 07-08 July 2016. During the discussion in the seminar, as per the suggestions received from Director (Implementation), Department of Official Language, Ministry of Home Affairs, New Delhi, HR Heads of all Divisions/Complexes are requested to ensure strict compliance on the following important points related to Official Language implementation in all the Divisions of HAL -

(1) The meeting of the Official Language Implementation Committee must be held regularly every quarter at an interval of 03 months under the chairmanship of the Administrative/Office/Divisional Head (General Manager/Executive Director of the Division/Project Head).

(2) It has also been suggested by the Director (Implementation) that 'Status of Official Language Implementation' must be kept as an agenda point for discussion in every high level meeting. The minutes of all high level committees should be prepared bilingually.

(3) All proficient and working knowledge employees and officers should be motivated and trained through Hindi workshops to work on LAN/WAN. To carry out Hindi work effectively, Unicode facility should be made available in their computers.

(4) The titles on the file covers used in all the departments/sections of the division should be compulsorily written bilingually.

(5) The data sheet/roster showing the knowledge of Hindi of all the employees/officers of the division must be kept updated twice a year i.e. in January and July. On the basis of this roster, the remaining employees and officers should be nominated regularly for Hindi training as per the annual training program approved by the competent authority. Also, once the number of employees with working knowledge reaches 80%, the official language will be introduced by the headquarters.

The office should be notified under Rule 10(4) of the Rules 1976. Also note that as per the guidelines of the Department of Official Language, Ministry of Home Affairs, New Delhi, the figures of trained employees/officers should be correctly reflected in the quarterly progress report.

(6) Letters received in Hindi must be answered in Hindi only. In order to increase the percentage of letters in Hindi, it has been suggested by the members of the Parliamentary Official Language Committee that acknowledgment of letters received should be sent in Hindi, for those letters which do not require answers.

(7) Under Section 3(3) of the Official Language Act 1963 under Rule 6 of the Official Language Rules 1976, the following documents will be compulsorily issued in bilingual form - (i) General Orders (ii) Notifications (iii) Press Releases/Comments (iv) Contracts (v) Agreements (vi) Licenses (vii) Permits (viii) Tender Forms and (ix) Tender Notices (a) Purchase/(Sale related) (b) Civil/other work related (x) Resolutions (xi) Rules (xii) Government papers (reports) presented in one or both Houses of Parliament (xiii) Administrative and other reports presented to one or both Houses of Parliament (xiv) Administrative or other reports sent to superior offices.

The consolidated report related to the above documents must be shown in the quarterly progress report and point-wise details related to the data must be maintained by the Official Language Section of the division.

8. It has been decided to include personnel of the following sections/departments under ministerial employees and officers - (i) All members of the Divisional Management Committee and their personal assistants (ii) All employees and officers of Human Resources, Finance, IMM, Security and Training Department.

For the purpose of inspection questionnaire of the Parliamentary Official Language Committee, all the above mentioned officers and employees will be considered as administrative part. Also note that official language posts will be created on the basis of this numerical strength. Updated data should be shown in the quarterly progress report, so that the situation can be presented correctly during the inspection of the Parliamentary Official Language Committee.

9. The follow-up action report related to the assurances given by the Division during the inspection conducted by the Parliamentary Official Language Committee should be sent within the stipulated period of 45 days.

10. According to the Parliamentary Official Language Inspection Questionnaire, Ministry of Home Affairs and Ministry of Defence, records of data related to periodic reports should be kept, so that we can easily consolidate the data in the correct form.

11. The constitutional provisions related to the official language including the annual program issued by the Department of Official Language, Ministry of Home Affairs, New Delhi should be circulated among the members of the Management Committee and the Official Language Implementation Committee and all the employees and officers of Human Resources, Finance, IMM, Security and Training.

12. The Official Language Section of the Division can put forward its proposal to obtain the required approval regarding the need for dictionaries to carry out translation work effectively.

13. All officers and employees should be motivated through organizing Hindi workshops/Hindi Pakhwada/Hindi Utsav by well-known and experienced faculty members/personalities in the field of official language Hindi. Apart from this, the difficulties of non-Hindi speaking employees/officers having working knowledge should also be removed through Hindi workshops.

14. The said report should be prepared by consolidating the brief quarterly progress reports from various sections of the divisions, so that the figures related to the said data can be presented during inspection.

15. Under Rule 11 of the Official Language Rules, 1976, banners/notice boards/printed materials/stamps/visiting cards etc. should not be prepared in English only. As per the instructions issued for 'A', 'B' and 'C' areas, the above items should be compulsorily prepared in bilingual/trilingual form.

16. Hindi books should be purchased as per the targets set in the annual program issued by the Department of Official Language, Ministry of Home Affairs, New Delhi. As per our administrative/technical work of the organization, suggestions regarding Hindi books can be received from the members of the Official Language Implementation Committee/Management Committee.

17. All employees/officers having working knowledge/proficiency in Hindi are requested to sign their signatures in Hindi as much as possible in official work. Apart from this, to increase Hindi correspondence, work should also be done in Hindi in LAN/WAN correspondence.

18. All reports related to official language should be sent on time. Apart from this, some important administrative phrases/official notes/designations/names of divisions etc. may be printed on the inside page of the file covers in bilingual form for easy reference.

19. According to the instructions issued by the Department of Official Language, Ministry of Home Affairs, New Delhi, the Official Language employees of the divisions should not necessarily be given any other work apart from the implementation of the Official Language.

20. Responsibility for compliance –

(1) It shall be the responsibility of the Administrative Head (General Manager/Executive Director/Project Head of HAL Divisions) of each office of the Central Government to:- (i) ensure that the provisions of the Act and these rules and the directions issued under sub-rule (2) are properly complied with and; (ii) Take measures to ensure suitable and effective investigation for the purpose.

(2) The Central Government may issue necessary directions from time to time to its employees and offices for due compliance with the provisions of the Act and these rules.

To ensure the above compliance, effective check points related to official language implementation should be made compulsorily as per the attached list.

(Ajay Kumar Tyagi) Executive Director (Human Resources)
